## RESOLVED (2026-11-10): the order of the 72 angels, including 5-7

**Computed from the text.** `tools/shem_names.py` derives the 72 roots from the consonantal Leningrad text of Exodus 14:19-21: v19 read forwards, v20 backwards, v21 forwards.
- Each verse checks out at exactly 72 letters.
- Result: 5 = מהש (Mahasiah), 6 = ללה (Lelahel), 7 = אכא (Achaiah).
- This is the canonical order of the Hebrew itself.

**Rudd's Goetia (Harley 6483) follows the canonical order.** Source: the Wikipedia Shem HaMephorash table, citing Skinner & Rankine, *Goetia of Dr Rudd*, pp. 366-376.
- 5 Mahasiah→Marbas (Ps 34:4), 6 Lelahel→Valefar (Ps 9:11), 7 Achaiah→Amon (Ps 103:8).
- All 72 Hebrew names in that table match the computed roots plus -el or -yah.
- So the Occultist Explorer pairing is correct.

**Harley 6482's sequence 5 Lelahel, 6 Achajah, 7 Mahasiah is a slip in that copy.** Mahasiah is displaced from 5 to 7, against the Hebrew.
- Its "doublets" are transliteration errors, not real repeats. Hahajah at 12 and 24 stands for ההע and חהו; Michael at 42 and 48 stands for מיכ and מיה.
- Its "Sirael" (3) is Sitael, סיט.

**Real repeats in the Hebrew** (distinct angels whose roots share letters):
- לאו at 11 and 17 (both Lauviah);
- והו at 1 (Vehuiah) and 49 (Vehuel);
- מבה at 14 (Mebahel) and 55 (Mebahiah).

**The angel seals:** Harley 6483 draws a circle for each of the 72 angels beside each demon seal. Each circle carries the angel's versicle (Latin psalm) and Hebrew name; the manuscript doesn't say whether it was meant as the reverse of the lamen. The designs themselves still need Skinner & Rankine's edition or BL images.

## CORRECTION (2026-11-10): Arbatel 1575, Olympic provinces (PDF p. 69 = printed p. 24; crop saved as `arbatel-1575-p24-provinces.png`)

**What the page prints:** ARATRON XXXXIX, BETHOR **XXXII**, PHALEG XXXV, OCH XXVIII, HAGITH XXI, OPHIEL XIIII, PHVL VII, total **CLXXXXVI (196)**.
- The earlier helper misread the total as 186.

**What it means:** the 1575 edition contradicts itself. The listed numbers add up to 186, but it prints 196, and the series runs 49, 42, 35, 28, 21, 14, 7 (falling by 7) only if Bethor has 42.
- So "XXXII" is a printer's error with one X dropped. **The 1575 edition intends 42.** Peterson's Latin text silently prints XXXXII.
- Later witnesses (Turner 1655, Sloane 3851, Rudd/Harley 6482) copied the 32 and changed the total to 186 to match: a later "fix" of the misprint.

**Verdict under the within-one-work rule:** the oldest witness's intent wins, so Bethor has **42**, total 196. The 32/186 reading stays on record as the later line of copies.

## ANSWERED (2026-11-10): do the 72 angels have seals of their own?

**No original set exists.** Every seal set is a later addition.

**Timeline:**
- **Jewish tradition** (Bahir, Rashi; Zohar II 51b): 72 three-letter NAMES OF GOD from Exodus 14:19-21. No angels and no sigils. The name itself is what gets written.
- **Reuchlin, *De arte cabalistica* (1517):** first makes them angels (-el/-yah), as products of God's will. Names and verses only.
- **Agrippa, OP III.25 (1533):** a table of the 72 angels with their verses, and no seals. But **OP III.29-30 give methods for making a spirit's character from its name**, including the kabbalists' method.
- **de Vigenère, then Kircher (1652-54):** names and verses only.
- **Rudd, Harley 6483 (mid-17th c.; copy 1712-13):** the FIRST seals tied to each angel, and they are made of the name and the verse in a circle.
- **18th c.:** the Toz pentacles given angel labels (Wellcome 4662); the Arsenal 2495 sigils, later published by Lenain and Ambelain (1951), whose attribution Ambelain himself retracted.
- **Modern decks:** derived from the above.

**Consequence for Annulus:** the most authentic "seal" of angel n is its Hebrew name (and verse), which is Rudd's form. A drawn sigil can be DERIVED from the name by Agrippa's documented method (III.30), labelled as derived. That is a deterministic function of the name, the same pattern as deriving a key from a spirit's number.
